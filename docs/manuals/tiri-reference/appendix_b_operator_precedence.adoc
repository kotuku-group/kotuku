== Operator Precedence
indexterm:[operators, precedence] This appendix provides a quick-reference for all Tiri operators, ordered from highest to lowest precedence.  Operators on the same row share the same precedence level.  See <<ch-operators>> for full semantics.

=== Precedence Table
[cols="1,4,2"]
|===
|Level |Operators |Associativity
|13|`pass:[**]`|Right
|12|`-` (unary), `not`, `#`, `~` (unary)|Unary (right)
|11|`*`, `/`, `%`|Left
|10|`+`, `-`|Left
|9|`<<`, `>>`|Left
|8|`&`|Left
|7|`..`|Right
|6|`^`|Left
|5|`\|`|Left
|4|`is`, `is not`, `!=`, `<`, `>`, `\<=`, `>=`, `in`, `has`, `≈` |Left
|3|`and`|Left
|2|`\|>`|Left
|1|`or`, `??`, `?!`, `? :`, `?? :`|Left (ternary forms right)
|===

=== Postfix and Suffix Operators

Postfix operators (`++`, `??` postfix) and suffix chains (`.field`, `[index]`, `()`, `?.`, `?[]`) bind tighter than all binary operators.  They evaluate immediately after the primary expression, left to right.

=== Self Referencing

`&name` and `+&&+` are primary expressions, not operators, so they carry no precedence of their own and accept the suffix chain above.

The `&` at level 8 is the bitwise AND operator.  The two readings are separated by adjacency: a `&` followed immediately by an identifier is a self reference, and a `&` separated from its operand by whitespace is the binary operator.  A self reference that opens a line also terminates the expression on the preceding line.  See <<sec-self-referencing>>.

=== Compound Assignment Operators

Compound assignment operators (`+=`, `-=`, `*=`, `/=`, `%=`, `..=`, `?=`, `??=`) are statements, not expressions.  They do not participate in precedence evaluation.

=== Parentheses

Parentheses override precedence.  `(a + b) * c` forces addition before multiplication.

=== Examples

[source,tiri]
----
2 + 3 * 4           -- 14 (level 11 before level 10)
2 ** 3 ** 2         -- 512 (right-associative: 2 ** (3 ** 2))
-x ** 2             -- -(x ** 2) (** level 13 before unary level 12)
'a' .. 'b' .. 'c'   -- 'abc' (right-associative)
x > 0 and x < 10    -- comparison (level 4) before logical (level 3)
a & 0xFF | 0x100    -- AND (level 8) before OR (level 5)
2 | 1 is 3          -- true: OR (level 5) before comparison (level 4)
6 & 3 .. 'x'        -- '2x': & (level 8) before .. (level 7)
3 ^ 1 .. 'x'        -- error: .. (level 7) before ^ (level 6)
flags has F1 | F2   -- has (level 4) consumes bitwise OR on RHS
a has b and c has d -- (a has b) and (c has d): has (4) before and (3)
0.1 + 0.2 ≈ 0.3     -- arithmetic (level 10) before approx (level 4)
x and y |> f()      -- (x and y) |> f(): and (3) before |> (2)
a ?? b or c         -- ?? and or share level 1; evaluates left to right
x ? a : y ? b : c   -- right-associative: x ? a : (y ? b : c)
----
